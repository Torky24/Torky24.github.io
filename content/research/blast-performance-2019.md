Blast-resistant design matters more as attacks with improvised explosive devices grow in number and scale, and cold-formed steel cladding is widely used when blast protection has to be built quickly. Working out how these systems hold up under a blast is usually slow and expensive.

In this paper, written with Shady Salem at The British University in Egypt, we applied several regression techniques to far-field blast tests of steel girt systems: multivariate linear, polynomial and random-forest regression, and checked each model with the R² score and the root-mean-square error. The idea was to lay the groundwork for a quicker preliminary blast-design approach that could carry over to other blast-protection systems.
